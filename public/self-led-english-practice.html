🤝 How to Use AI as a Thought Partner

AI is a powerful tool — but YOU are the thinker. Here is the key difference between using AI to learn vs. using AI to skip learning.

✅

DO: Use AI to Learn

Ask AI to explain grammar rules, give you feedback on YOUR writing, suggest vocabulary, correct mistakes and explain WHY, quiz you, and give you practice prompts.

🚫

DON'T: Use AI to Replace Your Thinking

Don't ask AI to write your essays for you, do your homework, or think through problems without your effort first. This skips the learning — and your professors will notice.

💡

WHY It Matters

When you use AI to think WITH you, you build real skills that stay with you. When AI does the work for you, you miss the growth. Your brain needs exercise just like your body.

✨ Examples of Great AI Prompts

"I wrote this sentence: 'She go to school every day.' Can you explain what is wrong and help me fix it?"

✨ Another Great Prompt

"I am a Spanish speaker learning English. Can you give me 10 vocabulary words about healthcare with the definition, pronunciation, and an example sentence?"

✨ For Conversation Practice

"Let's practice a conversation. You are a store manager and I am applying for a job. Ask me interview questions and correct my grammar after each answer."

📌 Remember

AI makes mistakes too! Always read AI responses carefully. If something seems wrong, ask again or check with your instructor. Your critical thinking is always the most important tool.

🎓 Using AI in College — What You Need to Know

📋

Always Check Your Syllabus

Every professor has different rules about AI use. Before using AI for an assignment, read your syllabus or ask your professor directly. Honesty always builds trust.

🔍

Verify AI Information

AI can give wrong facts. For research papers, always verify AI information with a real source — a book, a website, a database at the library. Cite real sources, not AI.

✍️

Use AI to Improve YOUR Work

A great workflow: write a draft yourself first — ask AI for feedback — revise your own writing based on the feedback. This is exactly how professional writers work.

🧠

AI as a Study Partner

Ask AI to quiz you before a test, explain a confusing concept in simpler language, or summarize a chapter so you can check your own understanding.

💬 ChatGPT Activities

🟢 ChatGPT — chat.openai.com

Build confidence by having a real conversation with AI! This activity helps you practice speaking naturally, learn from your mistakes, and feel more comfortable in English conversations.

  1. Open ChatGPT Go to chat.openai.com and create a free account if you do not have one.

  2. Start your conversation Use the prompt below to begin. Tell ChatGPT who you are and what you want to practice.

  3. Keep talking for 10–15 minutes Answer its questions. Ask follow-up questions. Try to write at least 5–8 complete sentences.

  4. Ask for error correction After your conversation, ask: "Can you list all the grammar mistakes I made and explain how to fix them?"

  5. Write in your journal Copy 3 corrections you received and write a new sentence using the correct grammar.

📋 Copy This Starting Prompt

"Hi! I am an English language learner at Urban College of Boston. I want to practice conversation. Please talk with me like a friendly tutor. Correct my grammar after each message I send and explain the correction in simple terms. Let's talk about [choose a topic: my daily life / my job / my home country / my goals]."

📋 Starting Prompt

This is exactly how college writing works. Write first, use AI for feedback, then revise. This is also how professional writers in every field work!

  1. Brainstorm with AI Ask: "Help me brainstorm 5 ideas for a paragraph about [topic]."

  2. Choose your idea and make an outline Ask AI to help you create a simple outline with a topic sentence, 3 supporting details, and a concluding sentence.

  3. Write the paragraph yourself Use your outline. Write every word yourself — this is YOUR paragraph!

  4. Get AI feedback Paste your paragraph and use the prompt below.

  5. Revise Make changes based on the feedback. Compare your original and revised versions — that is your learning!

📋 Feedback Prompt

"I wrote this paragraph for English class. Please give me feedback on: (1) grammar errors with explanations, (2) sentence variety, (3) paragraph organization. Do NOT rewrite it for me — just give me specific suggestions so I can improve it myself. Here is my paragraph: [paste your paragraph]"

⚠️ Important!

Always write YOUR OWN first draft before asking AI. AI feedback on your own writing teaches you much more than AI-written text ever could.

  1. Think about your goals What do you want to improve most? Speaking? Writing? Grammar? Vocabulary? Why does it matter to you?

  3. Follow the plan for one week Check off activities as you complete them.

  4. End-of-week reflection Ask AI: "Quiz me on what I practiced this week. Give me 5 questions."

  5. Adjust for next week What was easy? What was hard? Update your plan!

🤖 Claude AI Activities

🟠 Claude — claude.ai

Understanding subject, verb, and object is the foundation of all English sentences. Once you see this pattern, English writing becomes much clearer!

  1. Ask Claude to explain Use the prompt below to get a clear, simple explanation.

  2. Identify parts in 5 sentences Ask Claude to give you sentences to label.

  3. Write your own Write 5 sentences about your daily life. Ask Claude to identify and confirm the parts.

  4. Challenge yourself Ask for sentences with direct and indirect objects!

📋 Claude Prompt

"Please explain subject, verb, and object in English sentences using very simple language. Give me 5 example sentences and label the subject (S), verb (V), and object (O) in each one. Then give me 5 sentences for me to label myself."

  1. Learn the rules Ask Claude to explain capitalization rules (MINTS: Months, I, Names, Titles, Start of sentences) and punctuation marks.

  2. Practice correction Ask for a paragraph with capitalization and punctuation errors to correct.

  3. Compare answers Submit your corrected version and ask Claude to check it.

  4. Apply to your own writing Paste a paragraph you wrote for class and ask Claude to check only capitalization and punctuation.

📋 Claude Prompt

"Explain the MINTS capitalization rules to me with examples. Then give me a paragraph of 5–8 sentences with 8 capitalization and punctuation errors (no commas, missing periods, wrong capital letters). I will correct it, then you check my work."

  1. Question types Ask Claude to explain yes/no questions, wh- questions (who, what, where, when, why, how), and tag questions.

  2. Practice converting Ask Claude to give you statements to convert into questions.

  3. Comma rules Ask Claude to explain 5 major comma rules with examples.

  4. Comma practice Ask for sentences where you insert commas correctly.

📋 Claude Prompt

"Teach me how to write questions in English. Show me yes/no questions and wh- questions (who, what, where, when, why, how). Give me 5 statements and I will change them into questions. Then correct my work and explain any mistakes."

🔍 Perplexity AI Activities

🟣 Perplexity — perplexity.ai

Perplexity is great for learning because it shows you real web sources alongside explanations. You can see where information comes from — a great skill for college research too!

  1. Open Perplexity Go to perplexity.ai.

  2. Learn one tense at a time Ask about simple present, simple past, and simple future separately.

  3. Write your own sentences Write 3 sentences in each tense about your own life story.

  4. Get feedback Paste your sentences and ask for corrections.

📋 Perplexity Prompt

"Explain the difference between simple present, simple past, and simple future tenses in English. Give me a table with the verb 'to work' in all three tenses — positive, negative, and question forms. Then give me 6 fill-in-the-blank sentences to practice."

  1. Learn the rules Singular vs. plural, regular (-s/-es) vs. irregular (child/children), count (apple/apples) vs. non-count (water, information).

  2. Practice categorizing Ask for a list of 20 nouns and categorize them as count or non-count.

  3. Error correction Ask Perplexity to give you sentences with noun errors to fix.

📋 Perplexity Prompt

"Teach me about count and non-count nouns in English with examples related to daily life. Then give me 10 sentences with errors in noun use (wrong plural form, using a number with a non-count noun). I will correct them, then you check my answers."

💡 Microsoft Copilot Activities

🔵 Copilot — copilot.microsoft.com

These three topics are often skipped but they make a HUGE difference in how natural your English sounds. Transitions especially will improve your writing immediately!

  1. Prepositions of time, place, direction Ask Copilot to explain in/on/at for time, and location prepositions with a chart.

  2. Transitions Ask for a list of transition words grouped by function (adding, contrasting, sequencing, concluding).

  3. Sentence types Learn declarative, interrogative, imperative, and exclamatory sentences. Write one of each.

  4. Build a paragraph Write a short paragraph using at least 3 prepositions and 3 transition words. Ask for feedback.

📋 Copilot Prompt

"I am learning English. Please teach me: (1) prepositions of time (in/on/at) with examples, (2) a list of transition words grouped by type (adding, contrasting, sequencing, concluding), (3) the 4 types of sentences with examples. Then give me a short paragraph topic so I can practice using all three!"

  1. Understand the errors A fragment is an incomplete sentence. A run-on joins two sentences incorrectly.

  2. Ask for examples See clear examples of each error and the corrected version.

  3. Fix practice sentences Ask Copilot for 5 fragments and 5 run-ons to fix.

  4. Check your own writing Paste a paragraph you wrote and ask specifically: "Are there any fragments or run-on sentences? If so, where?"

📋 Copilot Prompt

"Explain sentence fragments and run-on sentences to me with 3 examples of each and the corrected version. Then give me 10 sentences — a mix of fragments, run-ons, and correct sentences — for me to identify and fix."

💼 Career Prompt: Practice a Job Interview

"I am applying for a job as a [job title] in [industry]. Please act as the interviewer and ask me 5 common interview questions one at a time. After each answer, give me feedback on my grammar, vocabulary, and communication clarity. Help me sound professional and confident."
